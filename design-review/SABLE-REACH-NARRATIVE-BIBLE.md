# Sable Reach Narrative Bible

Status: canonical expansion foundation  
Authority: `packages/content/src/narrative.data.js` is the machine-readable source for the records summarized here. The founding bestiary and cast remain authoritative historical floors; expansion records do not overwrite them.

## The promise of the setting

The Sable Reach is not recovering from an apocalypse. It is living through an ending that learned to proceed slowly.

Harvests remember fewer seasons. Roads forget one destination at a time. Children are born with adaptations their grandparents would have called curses and their parents call necessary. The dead remain socially active because many settlements cannot afford to lose their labor, testimony, or love. Ordinary life continues—rent, soup, jealousies, repairs, jokes—inside a world whose natural laws are becoming unreliable.

The vanished sun was not destroyed. It was divided into laws: warmth, growth, exposure, chronology, testimony, judgment, and other forms of light that once arrived together. Those laws can be reassembled. A complete restoration would end the decay, but it would also erase every organism, memory, road, community, and mercy that adapted to the long dimming.

The campaign's central question is therefore not “Will the hero defeat darkness?” It is:

> How much of a changed world may be destroyed in order to call the result restored?

The player is a necessary witness, not a predestined savior. Their alliances determine what the next age is permitted to remember.

## Moral law

No major character or faction is assigned good or evil. Moral ambiguity must come from comprehensible commitments and real consequences, not random betrayal.

Every major power must satisfy four tests:

1. It protects something no other power can replace.
2. It commits something the story will not excuse.
3. Its internal opposition is as intellectually serious as its leadership.
4. A player may support it for honorable reasons and oppose it for honorable reasons.

No ending is a color-coded morality result. The final state is composed from costs the player previously made other people carry.

## The three cosmic positions

### The Lucent Synod

The Synod is the procession of returning light: serene reliquary bodies, incomplete halos, veiled faces, white-gold cathedral metal, and daylight confined behind black glass. At a distance they are the most beautiful things left in the Reach. Up close, every inward-facing surface is scorched.

Their mission is sincere. Without renewed light, births, maps, agriculture, and ordinary time will eventually fail. Their miracles cure impossible diseases, repair ruined buildings, restore seasons, and return names that darkness erased.

Their horror is equally sincere. The Synod treats adaptive shadows, painful memories, local exceptions, and dissent as defects in a world-specification. Its most compassionate members still believe there is a threshold beyond which mercy becomes permission for corruption. Its hardliners insist an imperfect dawn is darkness wearing gold.

The Synod must never become generic holy villains. A player should repeatedly need what only it can provide.

### The Charnel Night

The Charnel Night is the organized opposition to restoration: impossible wet anatomy, door-like ribs, misplaced mouths, backward joints, black nacre, and halos made from negative space. It is visually terrifying even when offering shelter.

Its indispensable truth is that much of the Reach is alive only because darkness allowed life to become something daylight would reject. Charnel sanctuaries preserve altered children, embodied memories, impossible ecologies, and people the Synod would classify as errors.

Its unforgivable method is to cultivate hunger, mutation, grief, and terror because suffering creates the dense shadows in which its powers endure. Protection easily becomes possession. Sanctuary bodies can lock their doors. A demon may defend a village for centuries and still regard every resident as an organ.

The Charnel Night must never become a mass of disposable monsters. Every admitted form needs an ecology, an origin, a readable motive, and counterplay that expresses what it is.

### The League of Remaining Hands

The League is the mortal middle: lamplighters, physicians, delegates, smugglers, engineers, local rulers, and anyone trying to keep ordinary consent meaningful between cosmic powers.

Its doctrine is simple: no angel, demon, crown, prophecy, or arithmetic may spend a living settlement without informed consent. Its strength is practical knowledge. People who survive consequences understand costs that distant powers treat as abstractions.

Its failure is triage hardened into policy. The League quietly bargains away isolated villages, dangerous minorities, and inconvenient truths when the numbers appear to protect more people. It can become the most familiar form of evil in the setting: a reasonable meeting in which somebody absent is sacrificed.

## Principal cast

The first expansion cast contains twelve recurring principals. Each can become ally, antagonist, dependent, witness, or casualty according to the player's history.

- Arch-Lumen Seraphel Orr wants a mortal-sized sun and marks altered children for purification if mercy fails.
- Canoness Vael can cure almost any suffering and increasingly classifies identity, grief, and rebellion as symptoms.
- Deacon Halix restores natural law with his voice while consuming the names and relationships that prove change occurred.
- Saint Vespera shelters demons inside a second shadow from which they cannot leave without her consent.
- Mother Nacre gives altered refugees perfect sanctuary inside her ribs and retains the power to summon every guest home by force.
- The Prince of Thirteen Throats represents silenced peoples faithfully because he stole the voices with which he represents them.
- Wound-Scribe Keth prevents victors from erasing pain, but truth can be read only by reopening its injury in a willing body.
- The Maw Behind Mercy is a limitless predator learning restraint and the only known vessel that can complete a survivable sun.
- Enoch Vale keeps roads and lamps working while privately ranking which settlements he would abandon first.
- Sister Calve treats angel, demon, and mortal without testing innocence, then sells tomorrow's triage order to obtain today's medicine.
- Tor Vannic wants a sunrise with an off-switch and designs every safeguard so that only he can control it.
- The King of Ash Without a Country protects extinct communities from exploitation while ruling in the names of people who cannot remove him.

Founding characters may enter any cosmic storyline when their existing values create a genuine fracture. They are not reassigned merely to fill faction quotas. Maela Voss, Ysra Pell, Orik Senn, Vellin the Unwritten, and Gatewarden Nhal already have explicit alignment hooks in the canonical data.

## Quest-exclusive supporting cast

Recurring principals carry campaign arcs, but every admitted quest also owns at least one supporting character who appears in no other quest. This is a hard production rule: local people cannot be renamed scenery orbiting the same dozen celebrities. A supporting character normally carries an authored secret; an explicitly reviewed equivalent-depth variant may replace that field only when desire, fear, contradiction, owned decision, voice, alignment consequences, and quest function still provide the same dramatic depth.

The first thirteen are:

- Nima Sorn, a shade-cart engineer protecting the shadowless while measuring how to make their dangerous miracle permanent.
- Ilar Rook, a refugee demanding release from Vespera's shadow while sabotaging doors other prisoners might use first.
- Oren Lusk, keeper of three stopped calendars and the one relationship Halix most fears losing.
- Ilyen Doorborn, elected speaker for Mother Nacre's interior village and possibly an organ of the sanctuary she governs.
- Sava Quiet, a mute treaty auditor who hears unspoken votes but controls the cipher that records them.
- Mira Drel, a volunteer historical witness who suspects the archive's most painful proof of consent was authored after the fact.
- Fenn Vargo, the Maw expedition's tooth-counter, carrying his own condemned childhood as an emergency ration.
- Pella Vein, a dawn-engine stress tester who advocates distributed control while hiding a private override in her prosthetic hand.
- Solenne Marrow, an altered adolescent whose bones and skin obey incompatible skies and who refuses to become anybody's moral example.
- Davren Holt, a widower preserving grief as evidence despite his wife's final request to be forgotten.
- Roa Nullstreet, delegate for an omitted street who keeps it safe by feeding its address to an Unwritten Road.
- Bek Tallow, the hospice clerk whose donated tissue may have taught its living heart whom to deprioritize.
- Eri Cinderglass, a living claimant opposing rule by the dead while basing her own claim on a purchased ancestry.

Each has the same concept-master, transparent-cutout, static-model, and animated-model fields as a principal. These thirteen remain the foundation rather than the current roster ceiling. The accepted expansion register now exposes 70 character records. Wave 04 deliberately applies `equivalent-depth-without-secret-v1` to Senn Avir and Mora Quay; later writers must preserve that choice instead of fabricating a revelation. Leto Fain and Senn Avir have complete narrative identities but no visual brief, so both remain blocked from concept-art prompting until independently reviewed briefs exist.

## Main campaign spine

### Prologue — When Noon Came Bleeding

A vertical wound of daylight opens above Hearthmere. It repairs buildings according to remembered purpose while erasing the shadows of current residents. The player first encounters restoration as both miracle and displacement.

### Act I — The Procession of Necessary Light

The Synod arrives with cures, calendars, repaired roads, and a demand to inventory everything that daylight must admit. The player works beside Seraphel, Vael, Halix, and Vespera, learning that the saints are manufactured continuities rather than visitors from a morally perfect heaven.

The act ends when Halix restores seasons to three villages by consuming one relationship from the player's social history. The player cannot remain an observer: every future miracle will require a named bearer of its cost.

### Act II — Houses Inside the Horror

Mother Nacre opens a door in her ribs. The player enters a refugee village that has been safe for centuries and has never been told how much time passed outside. The Charnel Night's monsters become hosts, diplomats, archivists, and jailers.

The Prince offers a treaty through stolen voices. Keth offers the true history of the sun through transferable wounds. The player learns that mortals helped dismantle daylight to stop an older divine empire from using visibility as compulsory truth.

### Act III — The Arithmetic of Ordinary Survival

The cosmic war reaches roads, hospitals, ration ledgers, burial claims, and municipal lamps. The League's compromises become as consequential as miracles or monstrosities. The player discovers Enoch's abandonment list, Calve's biased living hospice, Tor's first failed restoration, and the King's unreliable electorate of ashes.

This act establishes that neutrality is a practice with victims, not absence from conflict.

### Act IV — Mercy Has a Mouth

The path to the dawn engine passes through the restrained Maw. Navigation occurs inside a predator that must be trusted without any guarantee that its hunger is cured. Previous treatment of altered life determines which companions consider that trust possible.

The Maw reveals that darkness is not the sun's enemy but one of its missing organs. A complete light without its dark half would produce certainty without privacy, growth without mutation, and testimony without refusal.

### Act V — The Engine With an Off-Switch

Tor can make restoration mortal, limited, and interruptible. Every shutdown design creates a different sovereign: one person, distributed settlements, a cosmic faction, or nobody. Earlier quests determine who is alive, informed, and trusted enough to hold a veto.

### Finale — A Sun Small Enough

The player does not choose between “good light” and “evil darkness.” They constitute a limited cosmology from incompatible laws earned through prior outcomes. Possible worlds include a mortal dawn, full Synod restoration, negotiated dusk, or refusal of the sun while the Late World continues dying.

Every ending must preserve one indispensable thing and make one loss impossible to dismiss.

## Quest canon and the 5,000-quest target

Five thousand accepted quests is a release target, not permission to inflate the game with errands. A quest is admitted only if it creates a distinct dramatic event that could not be renamed and moved to another location without breaking.

Every quest owns a story genome:

`primary mechanic + dilemma + location law + consequence channel`

All four coordinates must be authored. Exact genome reuse is forbidden. The primary mechanic identifier is unique even when underlying verbs—movement, dialogue, combat, crafting—reuse engine systems. Each quest also requires:

- a named giver with a contradiction and unique voice;
- a premise tied to a specific local condition;
- at least one objective beyond generic talk/gather/defeat/acquire/interact verbs;
- at least three materially distinct outcomes;
- one unique signature item with its own mechanic and lore sentence;
- a lore reveal that changes interpretation rather than adding trivia;
- a dialogue thesis that states the human argument underneath the activity;
- explicit downstream consequence identifiers;
- prose-similarity review against the whole accepted corpus.

The initial canonical tranche contains thirteen validated quests, thirteen quest-exclusive supporting characters, thirteen signature items, and nine linked main-campaign steps. It is deliberately small enough to establish quality law before production scales.

Each accepted record also carries an authorship proof: a unique visual setpiece, a fail-forward transformation, a dialogue constraint, a persistent world change, and a written reason the quest cannot be replaced by reskinning an accepted design. Outcomes write typed `authority`, `admission`, `memory`, `ecology`, `infrastructure`, or `obligation` state. Later quests declare which earlier state keys they read, so consequences become content inputs rather than congratulatory dialogue.

The operational batch, collision, state, character-art, and review contract is maintained in `SABLE-REACH-NARRATIVE-PRODUCTION.md`.

### Wave 04 v11 checkpoint

The current authored corpus contains 49 quests, 70 expansion characters, 39 expansion creatures, and 67 signature items. Forty-eight quests are release-attested across four closed batches; `relic_mirror_gave_birth_elsewhere` is the sole quest still in review. These are progress counts toward 5,000, not completion evidence.

Six agency contracts are explicit: four autonomous guest/follower contracts and two bounded contracts. The bounded contracts let Vael order already eligible records without authorizing anything and let Seraphel participate in defined material tests without transferring command, permission, office, remedy, or victim intention. They are authored narrative/operation boundaries, not evidence that companion runtime behavior has shipped.

The 5,000-quest allocation is a portfolio, not a template:

## No generic enemies

“Soldier,” “wolf,” “demon,” “angel,” “bandit,” or another stock noun is never sufficient as an admitted enemy record. Reusing skeletons, locomotion solvers, materials, and AI primitives is allowed; reusing identity is not.

Every creature requires:

1. anatomy that can be measured for concept and 3D work;
2. an ecology explaining how it persists outside combat;
3. an origin that creates story consequences;
4. a unique mechanic identifier;
5. one visible or audible cue;
6. counterplay expressed as a specific verb;
7. a narrative use that can complicate a quest;
8. concept-master, cutout, static-model, and animated-model fields with honest statuses.

The founding 178 remain intact. The first expansion admitted eighteen additional forms: six Lucent procession forms, six Charnel household forms, and six neutral living systems. Later accepted waves bring the expansion register to 39 non-generic creatures. Wave 04 contributes Boundary Gnasher, Foreword Cantor, Union of Last Breaths, Return Drinker, Dry Anchor Delegate, and Borrowed Silhouette Escrow; each retains a distinct ecology and counterplay contract.

## Swarm production workflow

Quest production runs in immutable batches. An author cannot approve their own work.

1. **Canon coordinator:** freezes world-state inputs, available characters, unused genome coordinates, unresolved consequences, and art/model readiness.
2. **Character owner:** writes desire, fear, contradiction, an authored secret or explicit equivalent-depth variant, voice law, relationship changes, and dialogue thesis.
3. **Region owner:** writes local history, ecology, location law, civic stakeholders, and what changes on the map.
4. **Mechanic owner:** writes the bespoke activity, readable state changes, failure behavior, and counterplay.
5. **Cross-review:** separate reviewers test canon, moral ambiguity, objective distinctness, outcome materiality, reward uniqueness, and similarity.
6. **Coordinator admission:** only accepted records enter the canonical module. Disagreement triggers revision. Rejected drafts remain outside the release surface.

Production starts with small batches and scales only after measured rejection rates remain healthy. A low rejection rate is not automatically success; it may indicate reviewers are normalizing repetition.

At large scale, similarity review uses a lexical prefilter to find likely neighbors, then exact shingle and semantic review. Every quest is still checked against the entire accepted catalogue, not merely its own batch.

## Art and 3D handoff

Every expansion character and creature has an explicit pipeline record:

- `conceptMaster`
- `transparentCutout`
- `staticModel`
- `animatedModel`
- independent status for art, static model, and animated model

MODEL MAKER reads those records directly. Empty fields display as unassessed or awaiting art; they are never inferred as ready. When an accepted PNG or model becomes available, its repository-relative path is linked in the canonical record and becomes visible in the same subject register.

An `awaiting-art` status does not waive the visual-brief gate. In the current Wave 04 roster, Leto Fain and Senn Avir are explicitly blocked because `visualBrief` is absent; neither may enter prompt generation, concept review, static modeling, or animated modeling until that authored brief passes review.

Concept art must translate story mechanics into measurable form. A beautiful design that does not expose its cue, counterplay, load-bearing anatomy, or signature contradiction is not production-ready.

## Writing prohibitions

- No morality meter disguised as dialogue.
- No faction whose cruelty exists only to simplify the player's choice.
- No surprise betrayal without a previously legible conflict of commitments.
- No quest differentiated only by names, quantities, rewards, or location labels.
- No lore delivered solely through encyclopedic monologues.
- No disposable enemy whose life outside combat is undefined.
- No reward item that is merely a stronger copy of another quest's signature.
- No claim that concept art, static models, animation, or downstream consequences exist before their linked artifacts pass review.
