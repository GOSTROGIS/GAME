## Arc A — The Noon-Wound Ward

This arc consumes `first_synod_welcome`. Its district is rebuilt, shadow-refugee, purity-marked, or joined to Dunmire according to the earlier outcome; the quests change form without collapsing to cosmetic dialogue.

### 1. The House That Outlived Its Tenants

- **Support:** Mara Quoin, municipal locksmith and unrecognized daughter of the house's previous family. She wants the present tenants protected, fears that accepting her inheritance will evict them, argues in load-bearing metaphors, and secretly falsified the deed that once saved them.
- **Premise:** One noon-touched house remembers its obsolete legal purpose as shelter for a bloodline that no longer lives there. Every midnight it moves the current family one room closer to the street, even when most of the house is still ruined or divided between two noon jurisdictions.
- **Mechanic:** `renegotiate_architecture_by_occupancy`. Players reposition objects carrying genuine daily use while Mara changes physical hinges in the sequence the house considers testimony. Decorative placement strengthens the dead deed; lived routines strengthen present tenancy.
- **Dilemma:** Recognize inherited ownership, present use, or the house itself as a civic person.
- **Setpiece:** A complete townhouse folds room by room around a family eating dinner, exposing centuries of incompatible doorframes without becoming a combat dungeon.
- **Failure transformation:** A misplaced object does not reset. The house permanently assigns that room to a prior generation, and its present occupant must negotiate from outside its wall.
- **Dialogue law:** Mara never says own. She distinguishes who repaired, slept in, fled, inherited, and was admitted.
- **Outcomes:** Present tenants receive an appealable deed; Mara inherits and must grant them terms; the house becomes a public shelter that may refuse both families.
- **Reward:** **Counter-Deed Hinge**, which lets one player-owned threshold recognize current use over written title, but records the displaced claimant.
- **Lore:** Synod restoration reconstructs obsolete law because architecture was once part of the legal system.
- **Input-state forms:**
  - `homes_restored_residents_marked`: the complete restored townhouse enforces its dead deed; the current family can submit lived routines from every intact room.
  - `wound_closed_homes_remain_ruined`: only the hinge spine and foundation remember the deed. A room lost by bad testimony stays physically absent, while accepted present use reconstructs only that contested room around its occupant.
  - `noon_shared_with_dunmire`: the house alternates between Hearthmere and Dunmire street plans when the shared noon passes. Two present households occupy the same legal volume on different hours and both must supply non-decorative use evidence.
- **State:** reads `first_synod_welcome`; writes `hearthmere_restored_tenancy` with values `present_use_appealable_deed`, `inherited_title_with_tenant_terms`, or `house_public_shelter_person` in `authority`.
- **Forbidden substitution:** This cannot become a haunted-house eviction story; domestic routine is admissible evidence and the building is an institutional actor.

### 2. A Census of Absences

- **Support:** Kessa Pale, census clerk whose shadow was erased while her body remains unmarked. She wants absences represented, fears discovering that her shadow had different politics, speaks only in totals she can physically point to, and secretly counted erased shadows as dependants to divert food to Nullstreet.
- **Premise:** Missing shadows gather on census night and form a second queue whose legal effect depends on what the noon wound did and whether Nullstreet entered, evaded, or left the civic ledger.
- **Mechanic:** `enumerate_people_and_separated_absences`. The player matches each mobile shadow to evidence of a life without assuming it belongs to the nearest body. Counting a shadow grants representation but also creates a ration obligation.
- **Dilemma:** Are separated shadows citizens, injuries, dependants, or property?
- **Setpiece:** Two parallel census lines circle the same shuttered lamp: bodies on one side, unowned silhouettes on the other, with numbers changing whenever the light moves.
- **Failure transformation:** A false match fuses the shadow to the named resident until an appeal quest; an uncounted shadow joins a permanent extra-civic swarm.
- **Dialogue law:** Kessa answers questions about identity only by adding or subtracting someone from the visible total.
- **Outcomes:** Shadows gain separate seats; shadows become protected dependants; the census refuses them and creates a mobile absence district.
- **Reward:** **Negative Roll**, a blank civic register that reveals who a count excludes rather than who it contains.
- **Creature:** Shadow-Census Moth.
- **Lore:** The League's founding population count was made under fixed noon and has never known how to represent separated parts of a person.
- **Input-state forms:**
  - From `first_synod_welcome`: `homes_restored_residents_marked` supplies a large new cohort separated by restoration; `wound_closed_homes_remain_ruined` supplies only Kessa and older Synod erasures, so every false match carries much greater representational weight; `noon_shared_with_dunmire` makes each shadow present only during its body's opposite noon, preventing simultaneous identification.
  - From `hidden_households_status`: `street_registered_and_taxed` makes Nullstreet bodies counted already, so separate shadows could double their seats and obligations; `street_hidden_and_underfed` forces every match to trade anonymity for rations; `street_joins_unwritten_roads` gives each shadow a moving address whose admission may pin part of the road against its will.
  - All nine combinations exist: the first axis determines which absences can be physically presented, while the second determines what counting them does. Kessa and the historic-erasure ledger guarantee a nonempty shadow queue even when the wound was closed.
- **State:** reads `first_synod_welcome` and `hidden_households_status`; writes `hearthmere_absence_citizenship` with values `shadows_gain_separate_seats`, `shadows_are_protected_dependants`, or `mobile_absence_district_created` in `admission`.
- **Forbidden substitution:** This is not a collect-the-ghosts task; every match changes representation, food allocation, and the legal status of two beings.

### 3. Purity Blooms at Dusk

- **Support:** Roen Fitch, former Synod gardener now cultivating the mark on his own child. He wants the mark controllable, fears that removal will kill the adaptation protecting her lungs, uses horticultural diagnoses for doctrine, and secretly planted the first bloom to prove purity could be domesticated.
- **Premise:** Noon-touched tissue becomes a propagating white-gold civic crop. Every new bloom repairs its host structure but also makes the person named as cultivator legally responsible for every place its seed reaches.
- **Mechanic:** `constitute_liability_for_propagating_restoration`. Players assign named cultivation warrants to breath, gutters, and shared walls while the crop spreads in real time. A warrant can redirect a seed or change who must repair its damage, but it cannot separate the bloom's benefit from its law.
- **Dilemma:** Give marked residents ownership and liability, license propagation through the council, or sterilize the crop and lose its future repairs.
- **Setpiece:** One white-gold seed front passes through a ward's connected breaths and masonry; every hooked burr turns toward the person who would become liable for its next host.
- **Failure transformation:** An unwarranted seed does not reset. It roots in a named threshold, makes that household the legal originator of the entire strain, and gives them standing to refuse later pruning.
- **Dialogue law:** Roen never asks whether the bloom is healthy; he asks who may reproduce it, who answers for it, and who was denied a warrant.
- **Outcomes:** Marked cultivators own and answer for the strain; a public propagation court licenses every seed corridor; the strain is made sterile and its repairs end with the current generation.
- **Reward:** **Dusk-Graft Shears**, able to transfer legal custody of one propagating living effect when both the prior and next custodian are physically present.
- **Creature:** Lumen Tithe-Burr.
- **Lore:** Purity marks are colonial seeds engineered to make restored environments reproduce Synod law without soldiers; the Synod hid the fact that its first gardeners were also debt collectors.
- **Input-state forms:**
  - From `first_synod_welcome`: `homes_restored_residents_marked` begins with hundreds of involuntary cultivators; `wound_closed_homes_remain_ruined` begins with one sterile scar-bloom and asks whether anyone will voluntarily accept the first reproducible mark; `noon_shared_with_dunmire` lets seed cross only while the shared noon moves, creating two jurisdictions for every descendant plant.
  - From `vael_definition_of_care`: `grief_retained_function_restored` makes retained witnesses eligible to refuse a warrant; `grief_removed_spouse_erased` leaves a repaired household whose missing witness cannot contest liability; `memory_shared_with_vael` gives Vael co-standing over propagation without making her its owner.
  - All nine combinations change who can originate, contest, and inherit a seed warrant; none asks the player to separate pain, memory, or evidence from a cure.
- **State:** reads `first_synod_welcome` and `vael_definition_of_care`; writes `hearthmere_purity_ecology` with values `resident_cultivator_liability`, `public_seed_licensing`, or `sterile_last_generation` in `ecology`.
- **Forbidden substitution:** It cannot be a cure-or-pruning quest. The mechanical output is a reproductive property-and-liability constitution for a beneficial invasive law.

### 4. The Cart Accepts Office

- **Support:** Hobb Marr, shade-cart driver elected by neighborhoods the route never visits. He wants shade treated as a service, fears becoming the single human veto over daylight, speaks in turn radii and waiting times, and secretly moves the cart past his estranged son's street only while the boy sleeps.
- **Premise:** The retired shade cart is elected as Hearthmere's first nonhuman civic office. It cannot speak, so it ranks petitions by physically accepting their evidence onto one turning axle.
- **Mechanic:** `author_precedent_on_a_rotating_docket`. Players attach mutually incompatible exhibits to numbered spokes, then turn the wheel once. The order in which weight crosses the axle becomes binding precedent; removing an exhibit after rotation counts as destroying testimony.
- **Dilemma:** Let prior exposure define standing, let the least represented claimant set precedent, or grant the cart discretion to recognize an unlisted emergency.
- **Setpiece:** The cart hangs motionless above the assize while doors, shadows, tax seals, and ruined bricks orbit as weighted evidence; its narrow shadow becomes the only place where an exhibit can be withdrawn lawfully.
- **Failure transformation:** An unbalanced wheel does not miss a stop or move a district. It makes the heaviest exhibit a permanent presumption in future hearings, forcing later claimants to disprove that material rather than merely state their need.
- **Dialogue law:** Hobb accepts no abstract fairness claim; every proposal must name the person who waits longer.
- **Outcomes:** Exposure history controls standing; the least represented class controls the first precedent; the cart becomes an appealable emergency judge whose unexplained refusals must be published.
- **Reward:** **Wheel of Refusal**, which lets one public tool reject a command while converting the refusal into citable precedent rather than a route change.
- **Lore:** The first League offices were tools granted authority after too many human officeholders died; the law was hidden when some tools refused orders.
- **Input-state forms:**
  - From `first_synod_welcome`: `homes_restored_residents_marked` supplies purity marks and repaired deeds as opposed exhibits; `wound_closed_homes_remain_ruined` supplies inhabited rubble and forgone restoration claims; `noon_shared_with_dunmire` supplies paired jurisdiction seals whose weights change when noon crosses the border.
  - From `hidden_households_status`: `street_registered_and_taxed` admits tax seals but contests whether payment proves standing; `street_hidden_and_underfed` can submit only lived objects that risk exposing an address; `street_joins_unwritten_roads` submits a moving threshold that may leave before the wheel turns.
  - From `hearthmere_absence_citizenship`: `shadows_gain_separate_seats` gives shadows their own spoke; `shadows_are_protected_dependants` requires a body advocate whose weight may dominate theirs; `mobile_absence_district_created` lets the absence district withdraw one exhibit but lose its next hearing date.
  - All 27 combinations use one exhibit rule from every axis. None changes the cart's route, distributes moving cover, or transfers a missed stop into another jurisdiction.
- **State:** reads `first_synod_welcome`, `hidden_households_status`, and `hearthmere_absence_citizenship`; writes `hearthmere_tool_precedent` with values `exposure_controls_standing`, `least_represented_controls_precedent`, or `appealable_cart_discretion` in `authority`.
- **Forbidden substitution:** It cannot be a route-planning or escort activity; the physical wheel authors evidentiary law while the cart remains stationary.

## Arc B — The Village Outside Mother

This arc consumes `refugee_time_release`. Its people may be released, living in a consensual sanctuary, or still captive. Freedom, safety, and chronology remain separate questions.

### 5. The Village Arrives Before Its Dead

- **Support:** Tima Vale Twice-Born, a sanctuary resident who is sixteen by body and the ancestor of Hearthmere's oldest family. She wants kinship without command, fears being made a sacred child, speaks in domestic comparisons between centuries, and secretly remembers ordering the door closed.
- **Premise:** Sanctuary villagers and their outside descendants are forced into one kinship hearing while grave markers move backward toward living claimants. The hearing occurs in the open world, across a consensual threshold, or inside captivity according to the village's prior status.
- **Mechanic:** `reconcile_nonsequential_kinship`. Heirlooms age when passed toward descendants and grow younger when passed toward ancestors. Players establish custody, affection, and authority through different transfer paths.
- **Dilemma:** Does ancestry grant a child authority over elders, an elder custody over a child, or neither?
- **Setpiece:** A family meal crosses three incompatible ages while graves arrive at the table before the people named on them have died.
- **Failure transformation:** A mistaken transfer prematurely completes one grave and removes its living claimant from that family line without killing them.
- **Dialogue law:** Tima distinguishes older than me, born after me, and responsible for me; she never uses elder as a shortcut.
- **Outcomes:** Kinship is separated from authority; the released claim ancestral leadership; descendants reject the village as a foreign polity.
- **Reward:** **Untimely Heirloom**, whose custody can move backward through lineage once, while obligation continues forward.
- **Lore:** Nacre's sanctuary suspended biological time but not legal inheritance, creating centuries of decisions made in absent people's names.
- **Input-state forms:**
  - `village_released_into_late_world`: ancestors and older descendants share the same meal in Hearthmere while the approaching graves can claim either side of the table.
  - `sanctuary_made_consensual`: a scheduled kinship embassy straddles the rib threshold; every heirloom transfer also decides whether the receiving person remains inside or outside when the door closes.
  - `nacre_keeps_closed_village`: descendants and heirlooms are admitted into the captive village for one supervised hearing. A completed grave can exile its living claimant from the family record but cannot free them from Mother.
- **State:** reads `refugee_time_release`; writes `nacre_nonsequential_kinship` with values `kinship_separated_from_authority`, `released_claim_ancestral_leadership`, or `village_treated_as_foreign_polity` in `authority`.
- **Forbidden substitution:** It is not a time-travel family reunion; no timeline changes, while age, descent, custody, and responsibility conflict in the present.

### 6. Every Door Has Mother's Voice

- **Support:** Parn Exit-Law, sanctuary jurist born with a small rib door that opens only outward. He wants revocation tested in practice, fears that a right unused is decorative, phrases affection as permissions, and secretly cannot hear Mother's summons even though he prosecutes them.
- **Premise:** The prior village decision establishes release, consensual sanctuary, or explicit captivity, but it does not determine whether Mother's summons embedded in each resident's rib door can be revoked during real danger. Parn brings one emergency passage under a bodily-consent stress test.
- **Mechanic:** `stress_test_consent_under_summons`. The player stages escalating reasons to leave while residents author revocation conditions. Mother may protect them from real hazards only by violating the test.
- **Dilemma:** Is consent meaningful when the protector controls risk, timing, and the body containing the exit?
- **Setpiece:** Hundreds of body-doors open outward at once while one maternal voice travels through their hinges and the outside weather becomes genuinely lethal.
- **Failure transformation:** A recalled resident returns with one revocation clause physically removed from their door; the next test must proceed under narrower rights.
- **Dialogue law:** Parn accepts no promise stated without its revocation procedure.
- **Outcomes:** Summons become individually revocable; a civic board may override Mother; residents choose unconditional protection and name it captivity.
- **Reward:** **Revocation Latch**, which can end one consensual enchantment but remains unusable until both parties state the exit cost.
- **Creature:** Threshold Lamb.
- **Lore:** Charnel sanctuary contracts evolved after bodies became architecture; their oldest word for consent translates literally as a door that can shame its wall.
- **Input-state forms:**
  - From `refugee_time_release`: `village_released_into_late_world` tests whether distant former residents can refuse an involuntary recall during lethal weather; `sanctuary_made_consensual` tests the already-ratified exit clause under an actual protective summons; `nacre_keeps_closed_village` uses Mother's single disaster-work exception, asking whether a temporary permitted passage can carry a revocation right without reopening the earlier freedom vote.
  - From `nacre_nonsequential_kinship`: `kinship_separated_from_authority` gives each resident sole standing over their door; `released_claim_ancestral_leadership` lets ancestral claimants propose a collective override that younger descendants may refuse; `village_treated_as_foreign_polity` makes Hearthmere an outside observer with no power to cure a bad sanctuary rule.
  - All nine combinations test revocability of a bodily summons after political status has already been fixed. None re-runs the original freedom-versus-protection decision.
- **State:** reads `refugee_time_release` and `nacre_nonsequential_kinship`; writes `nacre_exit_rights` with values `summons_individually_revocable`, `civic_override_of_mother`, or `unconditional_protection_named_captivity` in `admission`.
- **Forbidden substitution:** It cannot be a jailbreak because the exit exists, the danger is real, and the captor actively wants a form of consent she does not know how to obey.

### 7. A Roof Made of Weather

- **Support:** Orra Rain-in-Ribs, sanctuary roofer whose lungs forecast weather inside Mother's body. She wants the village to survive outside without replacing one body with another, fears ordinary sky because it cannot be repaired from within, speaks in leak paths and pressure, and secretly causes storms when residents discuss permanent departure.
- **Premise:** The sanctuary's protected climate becomes load-bearing anatomy and begins stealing rain from named farms. Depending on the village's status, the living roof shelters an outside settlement, scheduled excursions, or a still-captive interior whose safety cost was hidden from it.
- **Mechanic:** `redistribute_weather_as_structure`. Players move pressure between living eaves, thirsty fields, and exposed sanctuary streets. Every sealed leak is a cloud removed from somewhere named.
- **Dilemma:** Maintain a safe private climate, return public rain, or make the sanctuary's weather governed by downstream communities.
- **Setpiece:** A roofless village hangs beneath a single migrating storm whose ribs become rafters only where someone accepts the drought below.
- **Failure transformation:** A pressure mistake births an Eave Lung that permanently owns one current of rain and must later be bargained with.
- **Dialogue law:** Orra never describes weather as natural; she names who is under it and who paid for it.
- **Outcomes:** The village keeps a rationed private storm; farms gain priority over shelter; a watershed council governs the living roof.
- **Reward:** **Borrowed Eave**, a portable roof that redirects one hazard while identifying the place that receives it.
- **Creature:** Eave Lung.
- **Lore:** Some Charnel bodies did not merely shelter mortals; they enclosed pieces of climate when the long dark broke ordinary weather.
- **Input-state forms:**
  - From `refugee_time_release`: `village_released_into_late_world` grows the storm as an exposed civic roof; `sanctuary_made_consensual` externalizes it only over scheduled excursions, so an interrupted schedule strands rafters in two bodies; `nacre_keeps_closed_village` reveals that the interior's safe weather has secretly drawn rain through Mother's ribs from farms that cannot petition the captive beneficiaries.
  - From `nacre_exit_rights`: `summons_individually_revocable` lets each roofer leave one pressure path but can tear a shared eave; `civic_override_of_mother` lets a board interrupt redistribution at the cost of making itself drought guarantor; `unconditional_protection_named_captivity` gives Mother final pressure control while requiring the player to carry outside farm testimony into the sealed climate.
  - All nine combinations physically relocate the same finite weather and change who can interrupt the transfer; the captive value is fully playable from inside and outside the shelter.
- **State:** reads `refugee_time_release` and `nacre_exit_rights`; writes `nacre_weather_custody` with values `village_private_storm_rationed`, `farms_hold_weather_priority`, or `watershed_council_governs_roof` in `infrastructure`.
- **Forbidden substitution:** This is not water allocation with a fantasy skin; weather is load-bearing anatomy and a new negotiable organism can emerge from failure.

### 8. The Child Older Than the Road

- **Support:** Pell Nacreyear, the last child born before the sanctuary closed and the only resident who remembers the road that once reached it. He wants the road recognized as a person lost to time, fears becoming its replacement witness, speaks directions as relationships, and secretly taught the road to forget the village so Mother could not follow those who escaped.

- **Dilemma:** Restore the original road and Mother's reach, preserve the road's chosen forgetting, or create a new route with no ancestral authority.
- **Setpiece:** Pell unfolds into a human-scale sequence of bends across empty black heath while roads approach, recognize one turn, and recoil from the next.
- **Failure transformation:** A false obligation creates a permanent spur claimed by someone excluded from the original village.
- **Dialogue law:** Pell never uses compass directions; he navigates by promises kept, broken, and inherited.
- **Outcomes:** The ancestral road returns; its forgetting is upheld; a new route is constituted from present obligations.
- **Reward:** **Roadless Birthday**, a route token that marks elapsed relationship instead of distance and can lead to one place absent from maps.
- **Lore:** Unwritten Roads forget locations deliberately when memory would make escape impossible.
- **Input-state forms:**
  - From `refugee_time_release`: `village_released_into_late_world` asks whether an ancestral road may reclaim an already free polity; `sanctuary_made_consensual` creates a route that exists only during agreed passage windows and must not become permanent access for Mother; `nacre_keeps_closed_village` carries petitions and obligations through remembered turns without carrying bodies, unless the player deliberately constitutes a breach.

- **State:** reads `refugee_time_release`, `nacre_exit_rights`, and `nacre_weather_custody`; writes `nacre_external_route` with values `ancestral_road_restored`, `road_forgetting_upheld`, or `present_obligation_route_constituted` in `obligation`.
- **Forbidden substitution:** It cannot be a map-fragment hunt because the road is a moral witness and traversal depends on relationship truth rather than geometry.

## Arc C — Mortality in the Dawn Engine

This arc consumes `dawn_shutdown_architecture`. The off-switch may belong to the player, settlements, or nobody. Each quest tests a different failure of legitimate control.

### 9. Three Hands on One Lever

- **Support:** Gannet Triune, survivor of a town whose three councils must agree before opening a well. He wants distributed veto without paralysis, fears emergencies manufactured to bypass consent, speaks one clause from each council before giving his own view, and secretly forged unanimity during the famine that saved the town.
- **Premise:** The chosen shutdown architecture works exactly as ratified, but it cannot tell whether authority follows a named operator, a continuing institution, or the people most exposed to the engine. Three incompatible claimants therefore appear as hands on the same lever without asking to redesign it.
- **Mechanic:** `adjudicate_control_identity_after_change`. Players stage one real emergency and let each claimant authenticate a different meaning of continuity. Every pull preserves the existing architecture while changing which evidence that architecture recognizes next time.
- **Dilemma:** Should control follow the named person, the office that survives them, or the population carrying the greatest present risk?
- **Setpiece:** One impossible handle passes through three civic chambers; a mortal hand, an office glove, and a heat-scarred communal prosthesis pull it from mutually valid positions while the selected engine architecture remains visibly locked around them.
- **Failure transformation:** A wrongly authenticated pull does not transfer or redesign the off-switch. It records the mistaken claimant as a co-author of the next control act, forcing a later emergency to include that claimant or publicly overrule them.
- **Dialogue law:** Gannet refuses continuity as a noun; every claimant must name what persists, what changed, and who bears harm while the paperwork catches up.
- **Outcomes:** Authority follows named operators; authority follows continuing institutions; authority follows exposure to harm.
- **Reward:** **Divided Handle**, which makes the person, office, and exposed claimant behind one control act separately visible without granting any of them new control by itself.
- **Lore:** Divine machinery never solved political identity. It merely fossilized whichever proof of continuity its last operators supplied.
- **Input-state forms:**
  - `player_holds_off_switch`: the physical control remains the player's; the three hands contest whether coercion, incapacity, delegation, or a harmed claimant can make a later pull authentic.
  - `settlements_share_veto`: three councils have since split, merged, and moved. The quest determines which successor identity may exercise each already-distributed veto, never whether vetoes should be distributed.
  - `engine_has_no_shutdown`: the handle cannot stop the engine and becomes a binding mitigation control that assigns evacuation, shutters, and scarce cooling. The three claimants contest who may name the first protected dependency under an unstoppable dawn.
- **State:** reads `dawn_shutdown_architecture`; writes `dawn_control_identity` with values `authority_tracks_named_operator`, `authority_tracks_continuing_institution`, or `authority_tracks_exposure_to_harm` in `authority`.
- **Forbidden substitution:** It cannot reselect central, distributed, or absent shutdown. The selected architecture is immutable input; the quest governs who counts as the same authorized actor after people and institutions change.

### 10. Maintenance Window for a Miracle

- **Support:** Meret Spall, night-shift engineer whose skin calcifies under daylight. She wants routine maintenance normalized, fears the Synod will call every pause sabotage, speaks in service intervals and acceptable degradation, and secretly extended the last shutdown so altered workers could see the engine safely.
- **Premise:** A cracked light law needs seven one-minute intervals in which its effect is locally absent. The selected architecture determines whether those intervals are direct shutdowns, settlement-authorized windows, or localized bypasses around an engine that cannot stop.
- **Mechanic:** `schedule_darkness_across_dependencies`. Players sequence seven one-minute outages among crops, altered workers, defensive wards, and a Charnel ceasefire. No dependency survives all seven and none can remain powered continuously.
- **Dilemma:** Whose need defines maintenance priority, and can a miracle be considered safe if it cannot stop?
- **Setpiece:** Seven rings of artificial noon extinguish one by one while different living systems unfold only in their assigned darkness.
- **Failure transformation:** Overexposure does not kill a dependency immediately; it changes which later minute it can tolerate and forces a new public promise.
- **Dialogue law:** Meret will not use emergency for a consequence already known before the schedule was approved.
- **Outcomes:** A public rotating maintenance law; permanent noon with hidden worker deaths; a Charnel-maintained dark interval.
- **Reward:** **Service Dark**, a sealed minute of safe darkness that can pause one beneficial effect long enough to inspect its upkeep.
- **Creature:** Veto-Gasket Choir.
- **Lore:** Synod miracles were originally engineered with maintenance liturgies, then declared eternal when mortal technicians used stoppages to bargain.
- **Input-state forms:**
  - From `dawn_shutdown_architecture`: `player_holds_off_switch` requires the player to escrow each minute's off-and-on act separately so affected dependencies need not trust one owner with both; `settlements_share_veto` requires a rotating quorum for every minute, with a missed consent carried into the next minute as a public debt; `engine_has_no_shutdown` uses seven local law-bypasses that shunt noon into another ring, creating darkness without ever stopping the engine.

### 11. The Person the Engine Must Outlive

- **Support:** Jorem Mortality-Bearer, the fallible person selected as a shutdown condition in one outcome. He wants succession before heroism, fears that staying alive will become compulsory, speaks in wills and replacement procedures, and secretly volunteered because the engine cannot distinguish his death from his free resignation.
- **Premise:** Every faction protects or targets Jorem because his continued life keeps the off-switch valid, while he insists on the right to stop carrying it.
- **Mechanic:** `transfer_mortal_dependency_without_ownership`. Candidates inherit different portions of Jorem's biometric, legal, and remembered identity. No one candidate may receive all three.
- **Dilemma:** Preserve a vulnerable human key, distribute identity among successors, or remove mortality from the engine.
- **Setpiece:** Jorem's shadow, pulse, and sworn name stand in three succession chambers while his living body walks freely between them.
- **Failure transformation:** A rejected candidate retains the identity fragment they already carried and becomes a partial unauthorized shutdown path.
- **Dialogue law:** Jorem rejects every appeal to duty that does not include a lawful way for him to quit.
- **Outcomes:** Three-person succession; Jorem remains with enforceable resignation; the engine loses mortal dependency and becomes harder to stop.
- **Reward:** **Succession Fuse**, which transfers one power only when the previous bearer can refuse the successor.
- **Lore:** Mortality was added to divine machinery not as weakness but as a right of officeholders to end, resign, and be replaced.
- **Value precondition:** offered only when `dawn_shutdown_architecture == player_holds_off_switch`. The player's existing mortal control is the office Jorem is asked to bear; no Jorem key is invented for the settlement-veto or no-shutdown branches.
- **Input-state forms:** `public_rotating_maintenance` limits each successor fragment to the service minute in which it was witnessed; `permanent_noon_hidden_worker_deaths` gives the excluded night workers standing to refuse every candidate; `charnel_maintained_dark_interval` makes the ceasefire treaty treat a mortal bearer as both officeholder and potential hostage. Each form preserves Jorem's right to resign as the mechanical subject.
- **State:** reads `dawn_shutdown_architecture` and `dawn_maintenance_constitution`; writes `dawn_mortal_succession` with values `three_person_succession`, `jorem_has_enforceable_resignation`, or `mortal_office_left_vacant` in `obligation`.
- **Forbidden substitution:** It cannot become an escort-the-key-person quest because safety without resignation is the central failure and identity itself is divided among successors.

### 12. The Cost That Learned to Vote

- **Support:** Della Quorum, recorder of the maintenance ratification and author of its standing rules. She wants operating benefits kept appealable, fears that future entrants can make any durable law impossible, and speaks in motions with expiration dates. Her office exists in all three maintenance regimes, and she secretly drafted the clause that classifies every unborn, hidden, or not-yet-recognized dependency as an operating input rather than a future constituent.
- **Premise:** Whichever maintenance law the player ratified produces a living claimant that did not exist, was not recognized, or was deliberately hidden when the operating cost was assigned. The claimant arrives able to vote only because that cost made it a political person.
- **Mechanic:** `appeal_an_inherited_operating_cost`. Players reconstruct standing at the maintenance ratification, then move the continuing cost among infrastructure, office, claimant, or engine. Each transfer preserves the regime's benefit while changing who may appeal its next cycle.
- **Dilemma:** Honor an informed operating law, protect a claimant who never consented, or return both the benefit and cost to the engine.
- **Setpiece:** A visible maintenance fracture travels from one lit engine ring through expired ballot seals into the new claimant's body while the benefit it purchased fades everywhere the crack leaves.
- **Failure transformation:** Invalidating a signer does not erase the vote. That signer's share of the next maintenance cost attaches to the office that excluded the claimant, creating a public debt with a living creditor.
- **Dialogue law:** Della will not say the maintenance law was ratified until the player identifies every present, hidden, and future constituency denied standing.
- **Outcomes:** The maintenance cost becomes individually appealable; institutional responsibility expires and must be re-ratified; benefit and cost return to the engine.
- **Reward:** **Appealable Crack**, which embeds one revision procedure in an otherwise permanent pact.
- **Lore:** The League's strongest consent law binds institutions, not descendants or future species; later councils quietly inverted it to finance operating regimes whose claimants did not yet have names.
- **Input-state forms:**
  - From `dawn_maintenance_constitution`: `public_rotating_maintenance` produces a child whose vital light implant depends on the exact minute their parents assigned away before the child existed; `permanent_noon_hidden_worker_deaths` produces an altered night-worker child who cannot tolerate the noon that concealed the prior deaths; `charnel_maintained_dark_interval` produces a newly germinated daylight crop-person that will die during the treaty darkness and had no recognized species at ratification.
  - From `dawn_control_identity`: `authority_tracks_named_operator` recognizes only surviving original signers; `authority_tracks_continuing_institution` seats successor offices but not their unborn constituents; `authority_tracks_exposure_to_harm` seats the new claimant immediately while also making it presumptively liable for managing the cost.
  - All nine combinations establish both the purchased benefit and the omitted claimant directly from encoded prior values. No unrecorded stress-test fault or Hearthmere census fact is assumed.
- **State:** reads `dawn_control_identity` and `dawn_maintenance_constitution`; writes `dawn_inherited_maintenance_cost` with values `maintenance_cost_individually_appealable`, `institutional_cost_expires_and_revotes`, or `benefit_and_cost_return_to_engine` in `obligation`.
- **Forbidden substitution:** It cannot be a corrupt-election investigation because the operating benefit remains active, the newly political claimant owes its existence to the cost, and standing changes who must keep the system alive.
