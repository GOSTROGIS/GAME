# Sable Reach Narrative Production

## Mandate

The founding catalogue of 178 bestiary forms, 42 named characters, and eight origins is a floor, not a ceiling. The live release target is 5,000 accepted quests and has no character or creature cap. The target counts independently reviewed authored content, not generated rows or manufactured variants. A quest fails review if its dramatic work survives a noun swap.

The Late World has no moral scoreboard. The Lucent Synod is beautiful, necessary, and coercive. The Charnel Night is horrifying, protective, and predatory. The League of Remaining Hands is practical, democratic, and willing to abandon people by arithmetic. Existing characters may ally with any of the three when their personal contradiction makes the decision credible.

Production uses 417 immutable batches: 416 batches of twelve quests and one final batch of eight. A batch is a review and release unit, not a shared template.

## Admission contract

Every quest owns all of the following:

1. One story genome: primary mechanic, dilemma, location history, and persistent consequence.
2. At least one quest-exclusive supporting character with desire, fear, contradiction, secret, voice constraint, visual brief, and complete concept/static/animated pipeline fields.
3. One signature reward item with a unique world rule and a history that matters after acquisition.
4. A non-generic objective shape. Talk, gather, defeat, acquire, and interact may appear as connective tissue, never as the complete design.
5. At least three materially different outcomes. Failure transforms the situation and continues play; it does not merely reset the task.
6. One lore reveal that changes how a previously established fact can be interpreted.
7. One visual setpiece that can be identified without the quest title.
8. One dialogue constraint that changes how information can be obtained, not merely how a speaker sounds.
9. One irreversible or appealable world-state delta consumed by a later quest.
10. One written forbidden substitution explaining why the quest cannot be reskinned from an accepted design.
11. One branch-coverage proof for every state read. It must either author a mechanically distinct form for every value the upstream writer can produce or declare an explicit value precondition. With multiple reads, the proof composes one rule from every input axis and explains why each admitted combination is reachable.

The canonical validator rejects duplicate IDs, voices, primary mechanics, objective shapes, story genomes, signature rewards, supporting-character ownership, authorship proofs, incomplete state-value coverage, impossible read combinations, and high prose similarity. A coordinator also performs semantic review because two differently worded quests can still perform the same dramatic work.

## Swarm workflow

One narrative coordinator owns the canon ledger and collision registry. Three arc-owner agents work in parallel. An owner keeps an arc for its entire run so character voice, consequences, and local history remain continuous.

For each twelve-quest batch:

1. The coordinator reserves twelve unused genome rows and assigns four to each owner.
2. Each owner freezes a short premise packet before drafting. It names the local truth, moral cost, exclusive support character, reward rule, creature/ecology involvement, desired persistent state, concrete output values, and the full input-value matrix or explicit value precondition.
3. Owners draft complete records and dialogue/setpiece briefs. They may reuse a location only by advancing its state; they may reuse a recurring principal only alongside a new exclusive support character.
4. The other two owners independently cross-review. No author approves their own quest.
5. The coordinator runs structural, reference, collision, shingle, privacy, and registry gates. Any disagreement is revision, not a vote averaged into acceptance.
6. Accepted records become immutable. Continuations receive new IDs and explicitly consume earlier world-state keys.
7. The batch updates the canonical content module, narrative bible, character/model registry, item ledger, creature ledger, and coverage counts together.

## Lore ledger

Facts are stored as claims rather than a single omniscient paragraph. Every important claim records:

- who believes it;
- what evidence can be inspected;
- who benefits if it is accepted;
- which quest may contradict it;
- which world-state keys change its meaning;
- whether the player can publish, suppress, embody, destroy, or make it appealable.

A contradiction is not a continuity error when both claims have accountable sources. It is a continuity error when a later quest silently replaces an accepted fact without explaining who changed the record or the world.

## Character expansion pipeline

Every quest-exclusive character and every recurring principal enters one canonical record. There is no anonymous quest-giver tier. A crowd may exist, but anyone who owns a decision, reward, betrayal, testimony, or consequence is named and authored.

Pipeline states are explicit and honest:

`awaiting-art` → `in-review` → `accepted` for concept art, and `unassessed` → `queued` → `sculpted` → `animated` for 3D work. `refused` is available at either stage. A concept PNG never implies that a static or animated model exists.

The character register is generated from canonical content, not a hand-maintained showcase list. Every record exposes:

- concept master path;
- transparent cutout path;
- static model path;
- animated model path;
- family/faction and quest ownership;
- current readiness at each production stage.

When an accepted PNG or model becomes available, its repository-relative path is written to the canonical pipeline record in the same batch. MODEL MAKER then displays it automatically for Claude Design. Files remain immutable after acceptance; replacements use a new version.

## Creature and encounter law

There are no `skeleton`, `bandit`, `wolf`, `demon`, or other generic combat rows. Every admitted form requires authored anatomy, locomotion, ecology, origin, purpose, readable cue, unique mechanic, counterplay, narrative use, visual brief, and production pipeline.

A species may recur where its ecology supports recurrence. The encounter still owns a local population state, motive, altered behavior, environmental relationship, and consequence. Difficulty scaling changes numbers only after the authored identity is fixed; it never creates a new canon creature by adding health and an adjective.

## World-state scaling

Five thousand quests cannot branch as five thousand isolated binary flags. Outcomes therefore write typed state:

- `authority`: who may decide or speak;
- `admission`: who is included by a law, settlement, route, or ritual;
- `memory`: what is public, private, embodied, erased, or disputed;
- `ecology`: which form, resource, or hazard can persist;
- `infrastructure`: what physical service exists and who maintains it;
- `obligation`: which person or institution owes a specific future act.

Later quests declare the state they read, every concrete value they can consume, and the new state and concrete values they write. They do not add a congratulatory line and return to a default world. A line of alternate dialogue is not branch support: premise facts, available witnesses, mechanical constraints, or fail-forward consequences must change. When two prior outcomes cannot coexist, the later quest must declare a value precondition or stage that incompatibility as authored conflict.

## Art-production interface

Accepted narrative records create visual work orders in groups of at most six subjects. A work order compiles only canonical anatomy, role, mechanic evidence, faction language, and global art law. Family ownership, independent cross-review, immutable versioning, redacted provenance, raster validation, and honest MODEL MAKER readiness follow the same gates as the founding concept-art programme.

Art priority is:

1. recurring campaign principals and their signature creatures;
2. quest-exclusive characters required by the next playable content slice;
3. creatures whose mechanic cannot be understood from the existing family plate;
4. regional and aftermath cast in release order.

Writing may outrun illustration, but no record disappears from the register while waiting. `awaiting-art` is visible work, not missing data.

## Accepted foundation and Wave 02

The first tranche is the nine-quest campaign **The Returning Light** plus four side quests. It establishes three factions, twelve recurring principals, thirteen quest-exclusive supporting characters, eighteen non-generic creatures, thirteen signature items, and thirteen mechanically distinct quests. Each quest already carries the full authorship proof above and forms the test corpus for subsequent swarm batches.

Wave 02 expands three consequences from the campaign rather than introducing disconnected errands:

- the shadow-refuge or purity-mark aftermath in Hearthmere;
- the released, consensual, or captive village inside Mother Nacre;
- the ownership and failure politics of the dawn-engine off-switch.

Those arcs consume actual outcome keys from the first tranche and create their own exclusive cast, relics, ecologies, visual setpieces, and permanent world-state changes. Their twelve machine-readable records are now canonical, bringing the authored corpus to 25 quests, 37 expansion characters, 24 expansion creatures, and 25 signature items. This is progress toward the release target, not completion evidence.
