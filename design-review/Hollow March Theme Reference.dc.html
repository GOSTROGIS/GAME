01

Read this first

Handoff contract

This project is worked by several models and people in rotation — Claude Code, Claude Design, ChatGPT Codex. The rules below exist so that any of them can pick up work without re-deriving the look by eye, and without quietly inventing a second visual truth.

Rule 01

Tokens only

Never write a colour that is not one of the thirteen tokens in §02. If a new colour seems necessary, it is a token proposal — raise it, do not inline it.

Rule 02

Hairlines, not corners

Borders are one pixel of --line. Radius appears in exactly three places: circular runes, circular swatches, and 2px on kbd. Everywhere else, square or chamfered.

Rule 03

Gold is state, never prose

Gold marks labels, selection, accent and focus. Reading copy is bone or muted. Gold body text reads as fantasy pastiche and breaks the restraint the art depends on.

Rule 04

Three voices, three faces

Cinzel is the interface voice, uppercase and tracked. IM Fell English is the world’s voice, for description and dialogue. Inter is machine voice — keys, counts, telemetry. Do not cross them.

Rule 05

Label the maturity

The repo distinguishes authored, validated, habitat-valid, integrated, prototype, production and playtested. Any new surface claiming a capability states which of the seven it is. See §09.

Rule 06

Names come from the registries

Never invent a place, creature or item name in a mock. registries.js is the adapter boundary and contentGraph.js validates IDs. Invented names fail the cross-reference validator.

Active direction change · affects this system

Combat is moving from real-time to turn-based

The stylesheet in the repository was built for a real-time action game. Several of its components encode assumptions that turn-based play removes. Treat the following as under revision rather than settled — do not extend them, and do not delete them until replacements exist.

Under revision

The stamina meter and its .meter.stamina gradient — stamina as a continuously draining resource presumes real-time commitment.

Under revision

The action bar’s 1 / 2 / Space bindings and the dodge affordance — timing windows and invulnerability frames have no turn-based analogue.

Under revision

Line, arc and area telegraphs. Telegraph language is about reaction time; turn-based wants declared intent and resolution order instead.

Survives the change

Everything in §02–§05. Palette, type, surfaces, panels, codex cards, option cards and the ten encounter roles are mechanic-neutral.

Turn-based will need vocabulary this system does not yet have: an initiative or turn-order rail, per-turn action economy, committed-intent preview, and resolution log. None exist in styles.css yet. When they are designed, they belong here first.

Viewing the as-built system

Real-time combat grammar, as committed

Light attacks are quick and stamina-efficient; heavy attacks commit longer and scale with Might and Heavy Arms. Dodges spend meaningful stamina and grant a short invulnerability window. Ten encounter roles carry authored telegraphs, timing windows, counterplay and phases. Defeat returns the player to the last wayshrine with restored flask charges and a ten percent Sable Mark loss.

This is the state of the repository, retained here for reference. The project direction has moved to turn-based — switch the tweak back to see what that supersedes.

Membranes — translucent, composited over art

Every border and divider in the interface. Bone at eighteen percent — it reads as light catching an edge, not as a drawn line.

--panel

rgba(10, 14, 16, 0.9)

Panel fill for surfaces that sit over the world. Ninety percent opaque — the scene stays faintly present beneath.

04

Surface

Texture and depth

Depth comes from three stacked effects, never from drop shadows on cards or from rounded corners. Grain and vignette are always present, at low opacity, over everything.

Grain

Fractal-noise SVG at 8% opacity, stepped in two frames every .28s. Sits at inset:-50% so the drift never exposes an edge.

Vignette

A single inset shadow, 0 0 180px 35px at 85% black. Pulls the eye to centre and hides the viewport edge.

Glass panel

A 140° gradient between two near-blacks, one hairline border, a soft outer shadow and a 1px inner highlight. Blurred 12px behind.

Why the strip matters

All 178 creatures are canon-authored and habitat-valid. Twenty-one family encounters are prototype-playable. None have production models. A card without the strip implies a finished creature and will mislead the next author.

The pull-request template already requires stable-ID, provenance, GIS, authority, navigation and maturity evidence. This strip is that requirement made visible in the interface.

09

Honesty

The seven maturity states

The capability matrix intentionally distinguishes these. Using the words loosely is the single fastest way for four collaborators to end up disagreeing about what is finished. Each has one meaning.

Authored Canon content exists and is written. All 178 creatures are here.
Validated IDs cross-reference cleanly through contentGraph.js. No dangling references.
Habitat-valid Placeable in the atlas at a GIS-reachable location that suits its ecology.
Integrated Wired into the running slice — reachable through real UI, not a test harness.
Prototype Works, but the asset or system is a stand-in. All 59 current runtime assets are labelled this, which is why the strict-production gate fails by design.
Production Original authored asset meeting scale, pivot, socket, morph, clip and budget rules. Nothing is here yet.
Playtested Played by someone who did not build it, with findings recorded. Explicitly outside the current slice.

The rule that follows from this

A mock, a screen or a document may show any state — but it must say which. “Concept art alone is not a shippable world asset” is the repository’s own phrasing, and it applies equally to interface work: a beautiful panel with invented data is a prototype, and should be labelled one.
10

Where truth lives

Source map

Before changing anything, know which file owns it. Editing a derived surface instead of its source is how a second hidden content truth gets created.

File Owns
styles.css The entire visual system. This sheet is a view of it, never a replacement.
registries.js Canonical adapter boundary for enemies, characters, items, skills and regions.
contentGraph.js Whole-project ID validator. Run it before claiming any content change is done.
skillTrees.js 216 techniques, 144 actions, synergies and trials.
packages/content/ Bestiary, atlas, habitat, placement and scene data.
tools/worldgen/ Pinned deterministic GIS generation. Never hand-edit its committed derivatives.
tools/assets/ Asset validation. Fails undeclared assets and missing provenance — declare art before you reference it.
DESIGN.md Player promise, the three loops, visual direction, production priorities.
GAP_ANALYSIS.md Closed risks and next priorities. Read before proposing new scope.

Design boundary — keep this intact

The mechanical inspirations are broad genre patterns: use-based levelling, gathering and crafting loops, readable commitments, dangerous exploration, environmental storytelling. All setting names, lore, map data, quests, characters, interface work, code and generated art are original. The project contains no RuneScape or Dark Souls assets, characters, locations, text or trademarks — and nothing added under this theme should change that.

The bells are rung to keep the names, not the hours.
