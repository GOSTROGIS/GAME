# Sable Reach — 5,000-Quest Release Contract

## Release meaning

The release target is **5,000 accepted quests**, not 5,000 generated rows. A row counts only after it has complete lore, cast, item, state, authorship proof, and two independent semantic approvals. The founding catalogue and all earlier narrative are canon inputs, never templates to be reskinned.

Production uses 417 immutable review batches: 416 batches of twelve quests and one final batch of eight. Each batch has its own content hash, collision report, state-reachability report, reviewer attestations, character/item/creature delta, and Claude Design registry delta.

## Portfolio lock

| Portfolio | Accepted target | Required dramatic work |
|---|---:|---|
| Main, act, and cosmic turning points | 120 | Change a campaign law, ending, or cosmic capability |
| Faction and internal-schism arcs | 480 | Change doctrine, leadership, legitimacy, or alliance access |
| Character, guest, and follower arcs | 900 | Change desire, fear, contradiction, bond, refusal, or available ending |
| Regional stories | 1,200 | Depend on one ecology, history, or physical rule that cannot move elsewhere unchanged |
| Settlement stories | 1,000 | Change civic admission, service, ownership, memory, or obligation |
| Profession and systemic stories | 600 | Expose the moral life of craft, medicine, trade, travel, survival, and maintenance |
| World-state reactions | 400 | Exist only because one or more earlier outcome values changed the world |
| Relic and creature-ecology stories | 300 | Change custody or the way a non-generic form can live |
| **Total** | **5,000** | |

At least 2,000 accepted quests must read a concrete earlier outcome value. A continuation must transform that value into new dramatic work; replaying the earlier decision with a larger number is a collision.

## Non-repetition gate

A quest is refused if another accepted quest can perform its dramatic work after changing proper nouns. Every quest owns a unique value on every axis below:

1. primary mechanic and player verb;
2. moral dilemma and parties carrying its costs;
3. objective topology, including the order in which information and action become available;
4. location history and the way the location changes during play;
5. visual setpiece;
6. fail-forward transformation;
7. dialogue constraint;
8. persistent world-state key and concrete output values;
9. signature reward rule and drawback;
10. quest-exclusive support character's decision or testimony;
11. forbidden substitution;
12. relationship to earlier quests, including why it is a sequel rather than a replay.

String similarity is only an early warning. Passing a shingle or embedding threshold never overrides semantic collision review. Two differently worded escort quests are still duplicates when the cargo, route pressure, failure, and consequence do the same dramatic work.

## Character, guest, and follower law

There is no anonymous quest-giver tier. Every person who owns a decision, betrayal, reward, testimony, sacrifice, or persistent consequence receives a canonical character record with desire, fear, contradiction, secret, voice law, faction affinities, quest ownership, and concept/static/animated pipeline fields.

Every quest has at least one quest-exclusive support character. Returning principals may give quests, oppose them, travel as guests, or become followers, but cannot replace the exclusive support role.

A guest or follower record must declare:

- why they enter this quest rather than another;
- the mechanic they perform independently of the player;
- the action they will refuse;
- the priority they pursue when the player does nothing;
- the state value that changes their trust or availability;
- how and why they leave;
- concept master, transparent cutout, static-model, and animated-model readiness independently.

Followers are never generic stat packages. A companion may heal, fight, translate, testify, navigate, carry, sabotage, bargain, or witness only through a rule rooted in that character's history and contradiction.

## Item and artifact law

Every accepted quest owns one signature item or artifact. It must change a world rule, relationship, route, institution, memory, ecology, or future choice. It also declares custody, activation evidence, a cost or limitation, and what later quest can contest it. Numeric loot may accompany the signature item but never substitutes for it.

No two signature rewards share the same rule after nouns and magnitudes are removed. A stronger key, larger lantern, rarer sword, or differently colored seal is not a new artifact design.

## Creature and opposition law

No encounter row may be `bandit`, `wolf`, `skeleton`, `angel`, `demon`, or another generic body with scaled statistics. Every admitted form declares anatomy, locomotion, sound, ecology, origin, purpose, readable cue, mechanic, counterplay, narrative use, and production pipeline.

The Lucent Synod and related saints are beautiful, celestial, and institutionally terrifying: their evil is coercive restoration, painless erasure, compulsory truth, and salvation without consent. Charnel and other opposing forms are visually horrific and capable of predation, captivity, or cruelty, while still preserving truths and lives that full restoration would erase. Visual horror or beauty never assigns a moral scoreboard.

## State and branch coverage

Every quest writes one typed state in `authority`, `admission`, `memory`, `ecology`, `infrastructure`, or `obligation`, with at least three concrete values. Every state read has one of two machine-verifiable contracts:

- **all-values:** the quest authors a mechanical form for every value its upstream writer can produce;
- **value-precondition:** the quest is offered only for an explicitly listed reachable subset.

Multiple reads compose one authored rule from every input axis. The batch must prove each admitted combination is reachable or explicitly exclude it. Alternate dialogue alone is not branch coverage.

## Swarm production and acceptance

One coordinator owns the global collision ledger. Three arc owners author separate dramatic territories in parallel. For every twelve-quest batch, each quest is reviewed by two agents who did not author it. The author cannot approve their own work, and disagreement means revision.

An accepted batch is immutable. Sequels get new IDs, consume concrete state values, and must pass the full corpus collision audit. Rejected drafts and art remain outside release manifests.

## Claude Design interface

Every new character and creature appears in MODEL MAKER as soon as its canonical record exists. Missing assets remain visible as `awaiting-art` or `unassessed`; a concept image never claims a static or animated model exists. Accepted concept files are immutable and replacements use a new version.

Art is produced in family-owned groups of at most six subjects. Saints retain white-gold cathedral beauty, incomplete halos, veils, confined light, and ominous procedural anatomy. Charnel forms retain impossible black-nacre bodies, door-like ribs, misplaced mouths, backward articulation, and non-gory but severe horror. Every master and cutout receives independent canon and identity review.

## Completion evidence

The 5,000 target is complete only when all of the following pass over the complete corpus:

- exactly 5,000 unique quest IDs and 5,000 unique signature reward IDs;
- portfolio counts equal the locked table;
- at least 5,000 quest-exclusive support-character ownership assignments;
- no duplicate primary mechanics, story genomes, objective topologies, authorship-proof fields, rewards, state keys, or semantic collision findings;
- all read values trace to an earlier writer and satisfy all-values or value-precondition coverage;
- all references resolve and all state writes are typed and concrete;
- every character/creature record appears in MODEL MAKER with honest art/static/animated status;
- every batch has two non-author approvals and a matching immutable content hash;
- the full lore, privacy, raster, LFS, registry, and browser gates pass from a clean clone.

Until those checks pass, the production target remains active and interim counts are progress, not completion.

Current checkpoint: 37 canonical authored quests exist; 36 are release-attested across three closed batches, and one is queued in open batch 0004. MODEL MAKER exposes 49 expansion characters, 33 expansion creatures, and two autonomous companion contracts without claiming static or animated readiness.
